How this booklet works

Every chapter follows the same four parts. They match the four things you need to be able to do with numbers in the exam. Everything is worked through for you — read it, then use it as a reference when you revise.

1What does it tell us?Know what a formula means — not just how to plug numbers in. The key "why" questions about each formula, answered.

Why this matters. In the 7138 A-level, Papers 1 and 2 each have two quantitative questions and Paper 3 has one, set as 9-mark or 15-mark questions. You are expected to calculate and then use the result in your analysis and evaluation. A correct number on its own is not an answer.

The 3C + Q method for using numbers in an answer

Calculate
Get the figure. Show the formula and the working."ROCE = £260k ÷ £2,000k × 100 = 13%"
Compare
A number means nothing alone. Compare with last year, competitors, objectives or the budget."…up from 11% last year"
Conclude
Say what it means for this business in this context."…so the expansion is paying off"
Question
Evaluate the number: is it reliable? Historic? What's missing?"…but it's one year's data and ignores morale"

Exam toolkit: the mistakes that cost marks

Percentage vs percentage points. A margin moving from 15% to 9% is a fall of 6 percentage points — but a 40% fall in the margin.
Index points aren't percentages. 112 → 120 is 8 points but a 7.1% rise. Only from the base (100) do points equal %.
Pence and pounds. Dividend yield uses pence ÷ pence. 15p ÷ £2.50 is not 6 — convert first (15 ÷ 250).
Sign of a budget variance. AQA's formula is Budgeted − Actual. Don't trust the sign — ask "did this help or hurt profit?" to label it favourable or adverse.
Units for ratios. Current and acid test ratios are "x : 1" (or just a number); days are days; margins and ROCE are %. Always state the unit.
Rounding. Round at the end, not in the middle. Give 1 or 2 decimal places unless told otherwise. Break-even output rounds up (you can't sell 0.7 of a unit).
"Market share rose" ≠ "sales rose". Share can rise in a shrinking market while sales fall. Check both.
Leaving the number to speak for itself. Every figure you calculate needs a "which means…" and a comparison.
