Starter

So what is a mission statement?

A mission statement is a short statement of why a business exists and what it is fundamentally trying to achieve: its core purpose, beyond simply making money.
Tell me why: take any plaque from the starter. Could you tell, at the end of this year, whether the business had achieved its mission?
Not really. "Save our home planet" or "a happier world" can't be ticked off. It gives direction and values, but no number, deadline or owner. That's the gap objectives fill, and today's lesson is how to write good ones.
Notes. This is the bridge from the starter into the lesson question. Push for "you can't measure it", which sets up SMART.
Theory

From mission to action: the planning hierarchy

Level
What it is
Example: Kiln & Co (homeware retailer)
Mission
Why the business exists. Qualitative and long-term.
“Beautiful, well-made homeware that everyone can afford.”
Corporate objectives
Measurable targets for the whole business, set by the board.
Increase operating profit by 15% by March 2028.
Functional objectives
Targets for each department (marketing, operations, finance, HR) that add up to the corporate objective.
Marketing: raise online sales from 20% to 30% of revenue by March 2027.
Strategy
The long-term plan for how the objectives will be achieved.
Grow online sales and switch to cheaper overseas suppliers over the next three years.
Tactics
Short-term actions that carry the strategy out day to day.
A spring Instagram campaign, free delivery over £40, renegotiating the courier contract this quarter.
WHY
WHATmust be SMART
HOW
Notes. Build it one level at a time and ask "what would come next?" before each reveal. The last three reveals group the levels: why / what / how. The key point for today is that SMART applies to the two objective levels, not the mission. Kiln & Co reappears in Task 3, where students assign functional objectives to directors.

Theory

Tell me why…

One sentence each. Be ready to justify your answer. (Click a card to reveal.)

QUESTION 1
Why might a business with no clear objectives struggle to coordinate its departments?
Each department chases its own priorities, for example marketing pushes volume while finance cuts costs. A shared objective gives everyone the same end point.
QUESTION 2
Why do banks and investors care whether a firm has clear objectives?
Objectives show where the firm is heading and give outsiders a yardstick for progress. That lowers the perceived risk of lending or investing.
QUESTION 3
Why is "to make a profit" a weak objective?
It has no amount, no deadline and no owner. £1 of profit would count as success, so it gives no direction for decisions.
Notes. Cold-call for Q1 and Q2 and push for "tell me why". Q3 sets up SMART: ask which letters "make a profit" fails before revealing.

Where objectives fit.

The purposes on the right apply to both objective levels: whole-business and departmental.

1
Direction and focus
Turns a broad mission into targets that guide day-to-day decisions and resource allocation.
2
Coordination
Functional objectives all pull towards the same corporate target, so departments don't work against each other.
3
Measuring performance
Gives a benchmark to compare actual results against, so problems are spotted and corrected early.
4
Motivation
Clear, owned targets give managers and staff something to aim for and a basis for reward.
5
Communicating with stakeholders
Shows investors, lenders and employees where the business is going and how progress will be judged.

Theory

What makes an objective effective? SMART

S
Specific
States exactly what is to be achieved. No vague words like "improve", "grow" or "better".
Test questionWould two managers read it the same way?
M
Measurable
Includes a number or clear measure, ideally a starting point and a target.
Test questionCould we prove, with data, that it was met?
A
Accountable
A named person or function is responsible for delivering it and answers for the result.
Test questionWhose name is on it?
R
Realistic
Achievable with the firm's resources and the market conditions it faces: stretching, not fantasy.
Test questionGiven what we have, can it actually be done?
T
Time specific
Has a clear deadline or time period for achievement and review.
Test questionWhen exactly will we check?
Notes. Before each reveal, ask the class to guess the word for the letter. They will say "Achievable" for A, which leads into the next slide. The test questions become their self-check tool in the tasks.
Exam warning

A = Accountable, not Achievable

Achievable
This is what most websites, GCSE textbooks and older resources say. It is not what your specification says.
Accountable ✓
Use this in the exam. The "can it be done?" idea is covered by Realistic.
Tell me why: an objective could be specific, measurable, realistic and dated, but still fail. Why might having no named owner make that likely?
If nobody is responsible, everyone assumes someone else is dealing with it. Nobody monitors progress, nobody acts when it slips, and at the review nobody has to explain a miss, so there is little incentive to prioritise it.
Notes. Worth labouring. Ask students to cross out "Achievable" in any notes they already have.
Worked example

Building a SMART objective, one letter at a time

Parkside Cinemas: "We want more people to come to the cinema."
S
Which people? Which measure? → increase weekday evening admissions
M
From what, to what? → by 15%, from 8,000 to 9,200 a month
A
Who owns it? → the marketing manager will…
R
Is 15% plausible? → a new weekday-discount scheme and two extra screens make it stretching but achievable
T
By when? → by 31 August 2027
"The marketing manager will increase weekday evening admissions by 15%, from 8,000 to 9,200 a month, by 31 August 2027."
Notes. Take suggestions for each letter before revealing. Point out that R is a judgement in context: the objective itself doesn't "say" it's realistic, so you have to check it against resources and the market.

Task 5

Level 3 model answer

Setting SMART objectives would help Loaf & Ladle reverse its falling profitability, because it turns a vague aim like "do better" into a precise target that a named manager must act on. Food waste has risen from 9% to 14% of baked goods in two years. If the operations manager is made accountable for cutting waste back to 9% by August 2027, bakers at all seven sites know exactly what to aim for and progress can be checked monthly, which means that overproduction at a particular site is spotted early rather than at the year end. As a result, fewer ingredients are thrown away and cost of sales falls, without Priya needing to raise prices in a market where rivals are growing faster. Consequently the operating margin, which has slid from 8% to 5%, should begin to recover. This matters for Loaf & Ladle in particular because the bank wants evidence of improving profitability before lending £400,000 for Harrogate, and measurable, time-bound targets that are being hit provide exactly that evidence.
Highlighted = connectives driving the chain Underlined = application to the case

Level 2 (3–4)Some developed reasoning, but the chain breaks or application is patchy.
Level 1 (1–2)Knowledge with little development. Generic or a list.

Stretch

Are SMART objectives always valuable?

Think about the Loaf & Ladle mission statement. What could go wrong?

Unintended consequences
A manager chasing the waste target simply bakes less, leaving empty shelves by lunchtime and losing sales.
Hard to measure what matters
"Served by people who know your name" is central to the mission but hard to turn into a number, so it gets ignored.
Rigidity
If flour or energy prices jump, a fixed margin target becomes unrealistic, and chasing it pushes managers into short-term cuts.
Monitoring cost
A small family firm may lack the systems and management time to track several targets across seven sites.
Demotivation
Targets seen as unrealistic, like a big cut in staff turnover in a tight labour market, can demotivate the managers held accountable.
So… it depends on
how realistic the targets are, whether they are reviewed and adjusted, and whether they fit the mission rather than replace it.
Notes. Use this for fast finishers or as a whole-class discussion. The final card models the "it depends on…" judgement they'll need for 9-mark Assess and 15-mark Evaluate questions.
