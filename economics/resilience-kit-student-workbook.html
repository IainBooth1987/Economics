The Resilience Kit

An introduction to the basic economic problem. Four rounds. One bonus round. 100 points. Work with your team, record your answers below, then hand this sheet up when your teacher calls for it.

The scenario

In August 2026, the UK government's Cabinet Office announced a new campaign urging households to stock up on tinned food, bottled water and other basic supplies, in case of extreme weather or disruption caused by hostile states — part of an update to the country's civil resilience planning.

Read the real story on The Guardian →

Your team plays a household racing to build the best resilience kit — and along the way you'll meet three ideas that sit underneath almost all of economics.

Three key words

Scarcity — there is never enough of something to give everyone as much as they want.

Choice — because of scarcity, you have to choose what to have and what to go without.

Opportunity cost — the next-best thing you gave up in order to make your choice.
