Part A

Collect All Six Figures First

Work through each one below and record what you find. Save the analysis and the questions for Part B, once every figure is in.

Before you start: not everything below is a macroeconomic objective. Keep these categories separate as you go:

Objective / its indicator one of the UK's four core economic goals, and the data used to track it
Partial indicator gives you part of the picture for an objective, but not the whole thing
Policy instrument a tool policymakers use to influence the objectives — not a goal in itself
Secondary objective increasingly treated as a goal in its own right (e.g. fiscal discipline), but still ranked below the four primary objectives
Group 1 · The Four Objectives

...and what measures them

Economic growth, inflation, unemployment and the balance of payments are the four things governments are trying to achieve. Each has an indicator that helps track it.

1

Economic Growth (Real GDP)

Indicator → Economic Growth

The main indicator of whether the economy is producing more or less than before.

🔎 Go find it — ONS GDP bulletin Look in "Main points" for the sentence "UK real GDP is estimated to have increased/decreased by ___% in Quarter ___", and the annual % change.
2

Inflation (CPI)

Indicator → Inflation

Tells you how quickly the cost of living is rising.

🔎 Go find it — ONS CPI bulletin Look for the number next to "CPI rose by ___% in the 12 months to ___".
3

Unemployment

Indicator → Unemployment

Shows what proportion of people who want to work can't find a job.

🔎 Go find it — ONS Labour Market Overview Look for the headline "Unemployment rate" figure in the spotlight box or "Main points".
4

Balance of Trade

Partial indicator → Balance of Payments

Shows whether the UK sells more to the rest of the world than it buys, or the other way round.

⚠️ The trade balance is one important part of the UK's current account — it is not the whole balance of payments. Treat it as a useful clue about the fourth objective, not a full measure of it.
🔎 Go find it — ONS UK Trade bulletin Look for the total trade balance figure — a negative number means a deficit.
Group 2 · The Policy Instrument

Not an objective — a tool used to influence them

Interest rates are the Bank of England's main lever for hitting the inflation objective (and, indirectly, affecting growth). A tool, not a goal in itself.

5

Interest Rates (Bank Rate)

Policy instrument — not an objective

The Bank of England's main tool for controlling inflation.

🔎 Go find it — Bank of England Bank Rate The current Bank Rate is shown clearly near the top of the page, with the date of the last decision.
Group 3 · A Secondary Objective

Fiscal discipline — not primary, but increasingly a goal

Keeping government debt on a sustainable path isn't one of the four primary objectives — but recent UK governments have committed to "fiscal rules" (such as getting debt falling as a share of GDP), effectively treating it as a policy objective in its own right. Most economists still rank it below the four primary goals.

6

Government Borrowing & Debt

Secondary objective — fiscal discipline

Shows how much the government is spending compared to what it collects in tax, and how much it owes in total.

🔎 Go find it — ONS Public Sector Finances Look for public sector net debt as a % of GDP, and the most recent monthly borrowing figure, in "Main points".

Unemployment

— using the figure you recorded above
📓 In your book
What it measures

Complete this sentence: "The unemployment rate measures the % of people in the labour force who are ___ a job, but who are ___ and ___ for one."

Word bank: without · actively looking · available

Careful — someone who isn't looking for work at all (e.g. a full-time student) is not counted as unemployed, they're "economically inactive".

Why it matters

Bringing it together

Economists judge how well an economy is doing against four primary macroeconomic objectives: economic growth, low inflation, low unemployment, and balance of payments equilibrium. Your interest rate figure is a policy tool, not part of this scorecard — but government debt is a little different: modern UK governments increasingly treat sustainable debt ("fiscal discipline") as a secondary objective of its own.

  1. Using the data you've collected, which objective does the UK appear to be meeting best at the moment? Which is most concerning? Use your figures to back up your answer — for the balance of payments, remember your trade balance figure is only a partial indicator.
  2. Some economists treat fiscal discipline as a secondary objective, ranked below the four primary ones. Do you think it deserves objective status in its own right, or is it really just a means of protecting the other four? Justify your view.
  3. Is there any evidence of a conflict between objectives (for example, does pursuing low inflation seem to be coming at the cost of growth, or vice versa)?
  4. If you were an economic advisor to the government, what would you suggest as the single biggest priority right now, and why?
